== General Context
:doctype: book
:lang: en

In the current landscape of planetary research, the interoperability of data sets is an unavoidable requirement. Spatial programs and the massive publication of data volumes via the indexterm:[ODE]<<acr-ode,ODE>> service are on the rise. On the other hand, European researchers and archive services use the indexterm:[EPN-TAP]<<acr-epn-tap,EPN-TAP>> model to describe and disseminate their data sets, while the indexterm:[DOI]<<acr-doi,DOI>> (Digital Object Identifier) ensures the permanence and citability of numerical resources.

Finally, the Open Science movement requires that these data be indexterm:[FAIR]<<acr-fair,FAIR>> (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) footnote:[Wilkinson, M. D. et al. The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship. Sci. Data 3:160018 doi: 10.1038/sdata.2016.18 (2016).] in order to maximize their scientific and societal value.

=== FAIR Principles

The <<acr-fair,FAIR>> principles, originally defined by Wilkinson et al. (2016), are organized into four groups, each broken down into sub-criteria footnote:[Source: https://www.ouvrirlascience.fr/fair-principles/]:

[cols="1,6",options="header"]
|===
|Sub-criterion |Description

2+|*Findable*

|F1 |(Meta)data are assigned a globally unique and persistent identifier.
|F2 |Data are described with rich metadata.
|F3 |Metadata clearly and explicitly include the identifier of the data they describe.
|F4 |(Meta)data are registered or indexed in a searchable resource.

2+|*Accessible*

|A1 |(Meta)data are retrievable by their identifier using a standardised communications protocol.
|A1.1 |The protocol is open, free, and universally implementable.
|A1.2 |The protocol allows for an authentication and authorisation procedure, where necessary.
|A2 |Metadata are accessible, even when the data are no longer available.

2+|*Interoperable*

|I1 |(Meta)data use a formal, accessible, shared, and broadly applicable language for knowledge representation.
|I2 |(Meta)data use vocabularies that follow FAIR principles.
|I3 |(Meta)data include qualified references to other (meta)data.

2+|*Reusable*

|R1 |(Meta)data are richly described with a plurality of accurate and relevant attributes.
|R1.1 |(Meta)data are released with a clear and accessible data usage license.
|R1.2 |(Meta)data are associated with detailed provenance footnote:[W3C indexterm:[PROV]<<acr-prov,PROV>>-Overview: An Overview of the PROV Family of Documents, https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/ (2013).].
|R1.3 |(Meta)data meet domain-relevant community standards.
|===

[IMPORTANT]
====
FAIR and Open are complementary but distinct requirements, and PDSSP must satisfy both. Being FAIR does not automatically mean being Open: principle A1.2 above explicitly allows access to be gated behind authentication or authorization "where necessary," so a dataset can be fully FAIR while remaining access-restricted. For PDSSP data to genuinely support Open Science, it must therefore be:

* **FAIR** — findable, accessible (in the FAIR sense above), interoperable, and reusable; *and*
* **Open** — freely accessible without unjustified technical, legal, or financial barriers, and released under an open license, following the "as open as possible, as closed as necessary" principle.
====

The Planetary Surfaces Data and Services Pole (indexterm:[PDSSP]<<acr-pdssp,PDSSP>>) must therefore be able to ingest the free products provided by NASA/<<acr-ode,ODE>>, transform them into a format compliant with <<acr-epn-tap,EPN-TAP>>, assign them a <<acr-doi,DOI>>, and make them fully reusable within the Open Science ecosystem. This dual constraint—accepting a very specific input format and producing a set of heterogeneous outputs—requires a central data model that serves as a pivot between the different standards.
